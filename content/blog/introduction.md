I was born in 1983, in a small town in Brazil with about 20,000 people, at a time when almost nobody around me had a
computer. What got me into programming was wanting to change the endings of video games I played as a kid. An older
friend told me I would need to learn to code to do that, so at 12 years old I did, and I never really stopped. By the
late 90s I had my first job at an internet provider in my hometown, right when the internet in Brazil was still brand
new.

That early exposure to the internet and to Linux pushed me toward a career in software, starting with PHP web
development. In 2009 I pivoted into the financial market industry, spending years working with enterprise Java at places
like Deutsche Bank and the Brazilian Stock Exchange. Today I work as an Observability Engineer for a global payments
company in the Netherlands, building the telemetry and observability tooling that helps keep large scale systems running
reliably.

On my free time, I have been a volunteer technical leader for a game server project since 2015, leading a small team of
developers and community managers. What started as a childhood hobby turned into a long running project, and the game
itself has been running for 20 years without pauses.

I also have a strong personal interest in low level systems programming. C and Go are my languages of choice for hobby
projects, and I enjoy building tools from scratch just to understand how things really work under the hood, from
compilers to deployment automation.

This blog will mostly be about my personal projects and hobbies. Expect posts about game programming, C and Go
experiments, and sometimes some other random thoughts. The idea is to document and share part of my learning journey,
both to force myself to structure my process and to, maybe, make the journey useful to somebody else.
